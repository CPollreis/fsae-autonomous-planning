UMSAE Driverless — 24 Month Plan (June 2026 to June 2028)

Pass Driverless Technical Inspection and the EBS test, and score all three DV dynamic events at FSAE 2028. Maximum 400 points (Design 150, Acceleration 75, Skidpad 75, Autocross 100).

Governing principle. Reliability outranks speed. 150 of the 250 dynamic points are Starting and Completion points, which are entirely speed-independent. The plan optimises for passing scrutineering and finishing runs, and defers lap time to the final spring.

Phase 1 · Sep - Nov 2026 · M3 - M6 · CURRENT

Foundations, Procurement and Architecture Freeze
Outdoor window closes end of October. Treat as bench-only.
Every long-lead item ordered this phase arrives in time for the winter build. Anything ordered after December misses the summer 2027 testing window, which is the only full testing season before the target.
Mechanical
  • EBS architecture selection. UMSAE already has a working hydraulic service brake, so default to a pneumatic or pneumo-hydraulic intensifier design piggybacking on the existing hydraulics. Reserve electromechanical spring designs for teams wanting to avoid pressure-vessel certification overhead.
  • READ DT.3, DT.2 and the DSF.5 pressure-system documentation template BEFORE any CAD. The rules require every component to appear in the diagram with no simplification (DSF.5.1.1), so let the documentation requirement shape the design rather than follow it.
  • Size the actuator using the 200 ms actuation-time-derated force, NOT the static-rated datasheet force. One published thesis applies a 60% load factor because hydraulic actuators cannot deliver static-rated force inside the 200 ms window (DT.3.2.5.a). This is the single easiest way to design an EBS that fails its test.
  • ORDER the certified (PI/TPED) pressure tank and matching regulator EARLY. This is the number one EBS long-lead item: 4-8 weeks plus certification paperwork, and it carries a 5-year service-life expiry to plan around.
  • Steering actuator concept. Derive static AND dynamic steering torque from your own tie-rod offset, scrub radius and tire friction data, not a generic assumption, with an explicit safety factor plus a separate dynamic multiplier. Published worked example: 6.62 Nm static rising to 9.9 Nm then 18.15 Nm once factors are applied. Select the motor on CONTINUOUS duty-cycle capability, never peak torque.
  • Favour a back-drivable transmission (inline or rack-parallel ball screw, or a low-reduction quasi-direct-drive module). Back-drivability makes DT.2.2 decoupling and DT.2.1.1 manual steering nearly free. Only choose a high-reduction belt or gear drive if packaging forces it, and budget extra design time for the decoupling mechanism if so.
  • Preliminary EBS packaging study inside the Rollover Protection Envelope; sensor pod concept within the DT.2.3 envelope.
Electrical
  • ORDER the Remote Stop System immediately (Gross-Funk control box + vehicle module, SIL3, mandated hardware, RS.1.1). Nothing can drive autonomously without it
  • Order LiDAR, compute, IMU/GNSS. 4-12 week lead times, longer with customs
  • Shutdown circuit rework design: DS relay, Remote Stop relay plus bypass, EBS relay (DT.4)
  • DSMS, three DSSI, mission indicator and alert sound design (DT.1)
Software
  • Freeze ROS2 distribution (Jazzy recommended, matches QUT's current stack)
  • Stand up eufs_sim plus Docker dev environment and CI from day one
  • Fork rather than write: study QUT-Motorsport/QUTMS_Driverless and AMZ fsd_skeleton
  • Perception prototype against simulated data
Documentation
  • Start the DSF skeleton now. It documents the hardware, so it must be written alongside the build, not after it
Staffing
  • CRITICAL: recruit a dedicated EBS / mechanical actuation owner and a dedicated DV electrical safety owner. Neither role currently exists on the org chart, and both gate scrutineering
Gate
Architecture frozen, RSS and certified pressure tank ordered, all long-lead hardware ordered, simulator running a full sensor-to-control loop, EBS detailed design underway.

Phase 2 · Dec 2026 - Mar 2027 · M6 - M10

Winter Build I — Safety Hardware
Indoor only. This is what winter is for.
UTFR validated their EBS on the bench roughly twelve months before achieving full autonomous navigation. The safety hardware must be finished before the testing window opens, not during it.
Mechanical
  • Build the EBS and its bench rig. Cross-team evidence puts concept-to-bench-validated at roughly 6-9 months, so starting detailed design in Phase 1 is what makes the March gate reachable at all.
  • Staged validation: bench, then circuit, then fault injection.
  • Prove average deceleration above 10 m/s2 and reaction time at or under 200 ms (DT.3.2.5). Target margin under the cap rather than just meeting it; one published design targets 150 ms.
  • Prove at least half performance under single failure (DT.3.2.5.c).
  • Integrate into the existing hydraulic circuit WITHOUT disturbing brake balance. Re-verify balance-bar repartition; the shuttle-valve plumbing step consumed significant design iteration for every team that documented it.
  • Build the two Deactivation Points: red handle, marked Brake Release, must work with no electrical power (DT.3.1.2).
  • Build and bench the steering actuator and decoupling mechanism.
  • Instrument BOTH the actuator and the rack/wheel with independent angle sensors from the start. Do not trust actuator-side encoder telemetry as a proxy for actual wheel angle.
Electrical
  • Shutdown circuit rework installed and requalified with the EV team
  • DSMS installed: blue circular area over 50 mm, labelled DS, lockout/tagout capable
  • Three DSSI installed and verified visible from any angle at 1.6 m within a 3 m radius
  • Alert sound: 1-5 Hz, 80-90 dBA at 2 m, 8-10 s duration
  • Sensor pod fabricated and wired
Software
  • Perception: cone detection working in sim and against early real camera data
  • State estimation using ROS2 robot_localization. Do NOT write custom SLAM
  • Pure pursuit lateral control, longitudinal control
  • Hardcoded Acceleration path
  • Startup checks, continuous monitoring, and the DO.1.1 state machine
  • Inspection Mission (DO.1.3): spin drivetrain, sine-sweep steering, Finished after 25-30 s
Documentation
  • DSF sections 2 through 8 drafted as the hardware is built
Gate
EBS bench-validated to rules performance by end of March 2027. This is the single most important gate in the plan, and at roughly 6 months from Phase 1 concept it sits at the aggressive end of the 6-9 month cross-team range. Protect it: if EBS is the only thing slipping, pull people onto it from software.

Phase 4 · Jun - Oct 2027 · M12 - M16

Summer Testing — THE CRITICAL WINDOW
The only full outdoor testing season before the target. Roughly 24 usable weeks.
Every benchmarked team that succeeded did the bulk of its learning in real-world testing. AMZ's published lesson is explicit: simulation makes testing efficient, it does not replace it.
Activities
  • Staged EBS test build-up in speed increments with a large runoff area. QUT Motorsport crashed their car during an EBS test in December 2024 and ended its competitive life. Do not attempt a full-speed EBS test as a first attempt
  • EBS test to rules conditions: 40 km/h minimum within 20 m, stop within 8.5 m (DI.2.2.2)
  • Acceleration mission complete and repeatable
  • Autocross mission: online cone mapping with no prior map (DD.1.2.4)
  • End-to-end latency profiling. AMZ's ~300 ms pipeline latency became their hard speed limiter
  • Record footage for the Vehicle Status Video
  • Full-pipeline integration testing regularly, not just subsystem testing
Target volume
25+ test days, 100+ logged autonomous hours. Monash ran 2-3 sessions per week during their season.
Gate
Acceleration and Autocross missions completed autonomously, EBS test passed under rules conditions.

Phase 5 · Nov 2027 - Mar 2028 · M17 - M21

Winter Build II — Submissions and Refinement
Indoor. Documentation and regression season.
Documentation
  • Driverless Notice of Intent submitted. Organisers may cap driverless entries and screen on this document, so submit early and make it strong
  • DSF final, all 8 sections, strict formatting: team name, university and car number on every page, numbered pages, vector or 300 dpi images, searchable text, consistent component naming across every document
  • Budget for two to three rejection and resubmission rounds. Penalties escalate per rejection
  • Vehicle Status Video submitted. This is the real hard deadline because it requires a demonstrably working autonomous car
  • Driverless Component Summary (DS.1.2) and Design Briefing DV System Summary (DS.2.2)
Gate
All submissions in, no outstanding rejections.

Final Validation and Competition

  • Driverless Technical Inspection dry runs against the DI.1.2 checklist: sensor data sheets, local legislation compliance documents, remote stop control, tools, rules question copies
  • EBS test rehearsal
  • Speed tuning. This is the last 100 points and the last thing to touch
  • Confirm no post-inspection modifications beyond those permitted by DI.3.1
Gate
FSAE 2028, June. Compete.

Next 30 days

  • Order the Remote Stop System (Gross-Funk). Mandated hardware, no substitute permitted, and it gates all autonomous operation.
  • Order the certified (PI/TPED) pressure tank and regulator for the EBS. Long lead plus certification paperwork, and it gates the whole March 2027 EBS gate.
  • Recruit an EBS / mechanical actuation owner and a DV electrical safety owner.

De-scope ladder

If the schedule slips, shed scope in this order. Every step still banks points, because Starting and Completion points are speed-independent.
  • 1. Drop speed tuning. A slow car that finishes scores the same 50 Starting plus Completion points per event as a fast one.
  • 2. Drop Skidpad. It is the hardest event to complete cleanly and only worth 75. EUFS took four years to finish theirs.
  • 3. Drop Autocross. Keep Acceleration, which is a hardcodable straight line.
  • 4. Floor case: pass Driverless Inspection and the EBS test, complete one Acceleration run. That is 50 dynamic points plus up to 150 Design points, and it establishes the team for 2029 and 2030.
Never drop: Driverless Technical Inspection and the EBS test. Without them the car cannot operate in Driverless Mode at all (DI.1.1) and the entire entry scores zero dynamic points.

Top risks

EBS slips past March 2027
Impact: Forfeits the summer 2027 testing window, which is the only full season. Likely fatal to the June 2028 target.
Mitigation: Assign a dedicated EBS owner in Phase 1. Build the bench rig before the car integration. Treat the March gate as immovable.
EBS actuator sized from static-rated force
Impact: The system cannot deliver its rated force inside the 200 ms window and fails the EBS test, discovered only during dynamic testing when there is no time to redesign.
Mitigation: Apply an actuation-time derating (roughly 60% load factor) at the sizing stage. Bench-measure actual reaction time early rather than trusting datasheet values.
Certified pressure tank procurement or paperwork slips
Impact: The EBS cannot be assembled or bench-validated, cascading into the March 2027 gate and the summer window.
Mitigation: Order in Phase 1 alongside the RSS. Budget 4-8 weeks plus certification overhead and check the 5-year service-life date on arrival.
Steering coupling with backlash
Impact: Chalmers used a pneumatic clamp orthogonal to the column and only discovered the resulting steering-angle error, described as 'steering reduction', after a full season of on-track use.
Mitigation: Prefer a passively back-drivable ball screw or QDD module over an actively engaged clamp or clutch. Bench-test under load early, and measure wheel angle independently of the actuator encoder.
No owner for EBS or DV electrical safety
Impact: A software-only team produces a car that drives in simulation and fails scrutineering.
Mitigation: Recruit mechanical and electrical students now, in Phase 1.
Resource contention with the EV programme
Impact: UTFR's FSAE Michigan overall finish fell from 5th to 51st across 2023-2026 while pushing driverless and 4WD. The DV programme can damage EV results.
Mitigation: Agree vehicle access and shared-member allocation with the EV team explicitly and in writing, before the winter build.
Custom software detours
Impact: QUT lost roughly two seasons to custom EKF-SLAM before replacing it with slam_toolbox for 93% better mapping accuracy.
Mitigation: Fork proven stacks. Ban custom SLAM. Use robot_localization first.
DSF underestimated
Impact: A KTH master's thesis spent a full 30-credit semester reaching only ISO 26262 Concept Phase for the EBS alone. Rejections carry escalating point penalties.
Mitigation: Write the DSF alongside the hardware through Phase 2, not in Phase 5.
Summer 2027 crew never materialises
Impact: The critical window passes with nobody in Winnipeg to use it.
Mitigation: Commit named people before April 2027. Consider paid or for-credit summer positions.
EBS test crash
Impact: QUT ended a vehicle's competitive life this way in December 2024.
Mitigation: Staged build-up: bench, circuit, fault injection, then dynamic in speed increments with generous runoff.

A slow but reliable car that crosses every start line and completes one run per event banks 150 dynamic points. Combined with a strong Design score (150), reliability alone is worth up to ~300/400. Mechanical requirements

DT.3.1 — Driverless System Brake (DSB)
Required. Tractive System is NOT a brake system. All parts and mountings inside Rollover Protection Envelope. Manual braking must always remain possible. May be part of the hydraulic brake system.
DT.3.1.2 — Deactivation Points
Max two. Inside the DT.2.3 volume, near the DSMS or on top of vehicle between Front Bulkhead and Front Hoop near centerline. Near each other, protected from cone strikes, marked 'Brake Release', red handle, must work with NO electrical power, max two simple push/pull/turn actions with the order shown next to them.
DT.3.2.1 — EBS energy storage
EBS must ONLY use passive systems with mechanical energy storage. Directly supplied by GLVMS, DSMS, Remote Stop Relay and EBS Relay with no delay.
DT.3.2.5 — EBS performance
Reaction time (SDC open to start of deceleration) <= 200 ms. Average deceleration > 10 m/s^2 dry. On single failure should still achieve at least half performance. Must stay in stable driving condition while decelerating.
DT.2.1 — Steering actuation
Manual steering must be possible with DSMS Off with no other steps. Steering actuation only possible when Ready to Drive. Steering may stay active during an Emergency Brake Maneuver while moving.
DT.2.2 — Actuator decoupling
If actuators are disconnected for Manual Mode: no parts removed, mechanism must not block manual operation in any position, must lock securely in both positions.
DT.2.3 — Sensor and electronics mounting
All DV components including sensors and cameras must sit inside the volume formed by the outside surfaces of chassis and tires, or in the permitted Aerodynamic Device locations (T.7.5-T.7.7).

Electrical requirements

DT.1.1 — Driverless System Master Switch (DSMS)
Meets T.9.3 config/location. Direct acting, not through relay or logic. Centered in a fully blue circular area >50 mm diameter, labeled 'DS', lockout/tagout capable in OFF. When OFF: no steering/braking/propulsion actuation, car pushable, car drivable in Manual Mode. Sensors and processing may still run.
DT.1.2 — Tractive System Activation Button
Located near the Tractive System Master Switch. Puts car in TS Active from outside the vehicle. Does nothing when DSMS is OFF.
DT.1.3 — Driverless System Status Indicators (DSSI)
THREE indicators: one each side behind cockpit (160 mm below Main Hoop top to 600 mm above ground), one at rear on centerline (160 mm below Main Hoop top to 100 mm above brake light). At least one visible from any angle at 1.6 m height within 3 m radius. Black background, >=15 cm^2 shining surface. Bare LEDs <=20 mm apart; single LED line >=150 mm long. No other function.
DO.1.1.3 — DSSI states
Off=Off, Ready=Yellow Continuous, Driving=Yellow Flashing, Finished=Blue Continuous, Emergency=Blue Flashing.
DT.1.4 — Driverless Alert Sound
On Emergency status: intermittent tone 1-5 Hz at 50% duty, 80-90 dBA fast weighting within 2 m radius, for 8-10 s.
DO.1.4 — Driverless Mission Indicator
Shows selected mission, easily readable, on the dash or near the DSMS.
DT.3.3 / RS — Remote Stop System (RSS)
MANDATED HARDWARE: Gross-Funk GmbH control box + vehicle module, latest revision with E-Key. SIL3 (EN61508) certified, 25 mW, 12-24 V (0.26 A @ 12 V), 450 g, IP20 receiver / IP65 sender. Vehicle module has an NO relay in the shutdown circuit, opens on shutdown, signal loss, and power loss, 4 A max. CANopen at Node-ID 0x011, 500 kbps at competition, set by DIP switches. Antenna must be unobstructed. LONG-LEAD PROCUREMENT ITEM.
DT.4 — Shutdown circuit additions
Three additions: (1) Driverless System Relay, normally open permissive, closes when DS checks pass. (2) Remote Stop Relay, in series with shutdown buttons, bypassed by a normally-closed relay in Manual Mode; bypass relay directly supplied by DSMS with safety-certified forcibly guided or mirrored NO contact in series with DSMS. (3) EBS Relay, fail-open, coil in parallel with the Isolation Relays.
DO.2.2 — Wireless restrictions
Between start and finish line: no wireless TO the vehicle. One-way telemetry FROM the vehicle is allowed. The Remote Stop Control Box is the only device that may command the car.

Software requirements

DO.1.2.1 — Required missions
Must implement at minimum: Inspection, Manual Driving, EBS Test, Acceleration, Skidpad, Autocross. Trackdrive optional for 2026, expected later.
DO.1.2.2 — Mission selection
Must not use an external device. Needs onboard selection (dash/rotary switch).
DT.3.1.3 — Startup check and monitoring
A Startup Check must prove the DSB builds brake pressure as expected before Ready is possible. After that, DSB and its signals must be CONTINUOUSLY monitored for failures.
DT.3.2.3 — Safe State logic
Go to Safe State if EBS functionality cannot be ensured, or if one additional single point of failure would cause total loss of braking. Safe State = standstill + brakes engaged + open shutdown circuit.
DO.1.1 — State machine
Driverless System Status determined by the DO.1.1.1 flowchart: Off, Ready, Driving, Finished, Emergency.
DD.1.2.4 — No prior map
No map data is given. No artificial landmarks or beacons may be placed. Track must be discovered online from cones alone.
DD.3.2.2 — Start timing
Ready to Drive only via the 'Go' signal from the Remote Stop Control Box, after >=5 s in Ready. Run must start within 30 s of Go or it is DNF.
DD.3.1.5 — Staging timing
Must enter Driverless System Ready within 1 minute of staging or the team may be sent back.
DD.2.1.3 — Unsafe Stop
DQ if the car does not stop in the specified area OR does not reach Finished within 30 s of stopping.

Required submissions

Driverless Notice of Intent (Roadmap / DA)
Opt-in to the Driverless Class. Must include anticipated milestones, overview of sensors, compute and software stack, actuation method, safety strategy, and team structure. Signed by Team Captain and Faculty Advisor. Organizers may use it to screen entries when slots are limited.
Driverless Systems Form (DSF) (DA.2.2.1)
Eight sections: DSF.1 Overall/formatting, DSF.2 Actuator Power Supply Schematic, DSF.3 Continuous Monitoring Flowchart, DSF.4 High Level Block Diagram, DSF.5 Pressure System Overview, DSF.6 Startup Checks Flowchart, DSF.7 Mechanical Assembly Drawings, DSF.8 CAN Database File. Strict formatting: team/university/car number and section name on every page, numbered pages, vector or 300dpi images, searchable text, consistent component naming across all documents.
Vehicle Status Video (DA.2.2.2)
Video showing the vehicle operating in Driverless Mode, submitted before competition. THIS IS THE REAL DEADLINE: it forces a working autonomous car months before the event.
Driverless Component Summary (DS.1.2)
Itemized list of components that are part of the DV system and serve no other purpose. Excludes them from the Electric Cost Event.
Design Briefing / DV System Summary (DS.2.2)
Driverless System Summary included in the Design Briefing template (S.4.3).
